Growing squares until they jam

Each box starts with __N__ tiny squares at random places and angles. A rule picks which squares grow each step, how, and who gets pushed. When nothing can grow, every square shrinks to the smallest one, and that state is squeezed into the tightest box it allows. Smaller box side is better; the last panel is our best known packing for n = __N__.

Negative space is shaded by clearance: how far each empty point is from the nearest square or wall. Thin cracks are pale, wide gaps are dark, and red marks points with room for a disc as wide as the smallest square, a hole that could almost take another square. The empty share is 1 − total square area / box area; for unit squares that is 1 − n / L².

The animation shows growth in a unit box. At the end each panel switches to the squeezed packing of unit squares, drawn at the same scale as the record. "Jammed side" is 1 / smallest square when growth stops; "squeezed side" is after the FIRE squeeze.
